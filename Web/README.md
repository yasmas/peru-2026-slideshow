# Peru 2026 · Vintage Rides Andes

Open `index.html` in a modern browser, choose **Quick Show** (the default) or **Full Show**, then press the Play button below the picture. Everything needed for playback is inside this `Web` directory; an internet connection is not required. For a local HTTP preview, run `python3 Web/tools/serve.py` from the parent `2026-09-28 Peru` directory and visit `http://127.0.0.1:8751/`. This server supports byte-range requests required for reliable Safari video playback; plain `python3 -m http.server` does not.

Use **Space** to play or pause, **←/→** to move between frames, **F** for clean media-only fullscreen, and **M** to toggle music. The **ROUTE** button jumps to a chapter. The small mode button switches versions while paused and restarts at frame 1. Videos use 25% volume while music is capped at 75% during them, except the flute clip, where background music is silent while its audio plays. Photos and videos are fitted to the frame. **Full Show** runs **19:38** across 250 screens. **Quick Show** runs about **12:38** across 143 screens and includes only media rated 3 stars or higher. When only one photo in a pair qualifies, it fills the frame alone. Ordinary solo iPhone photos show for 3 seconds and other solo photos for 4 seconds; selected scenic shots and opening/place titles keep their longer holds. Returning to the first screen with the left arrow or timeline pauses the show and cues the opening track at 0:00.

## Media and sequence

The opening poster (`b2c6cd50-757d-44f7-8862-a703852ff561.heic`) is followed by the Arequipa landing clip. The former slides 8–12 now follow the landing, with the Arequipa title on the first of them; the former slide 3 follows the former slide 6. The camera photos follow their embedded `DateTimeOriginal` metadata, regardless of file modification time. The camera clips have import dates in their QuickTime metadata, so their placement was recovered from camera sequence numbers, nearby GPS locations, and pictured scenes. The opening wing clip is trimmed to 4.5 seconds from its middle section. The added WhatsApp road video is placed on October 7 between Macusani and Lake Titicaca and plays for 55 seconds. Other long clips play for at most 20 seconds; the shortened riding clip plays for 7 seconds. The shorter vertical flute video remains; the longer horizontal flute video and several selected photos were removed from the sequence. The Moray terraces photo has no original capture timestamp, so its scene determines placement beside the nearby Maras visit on October 3. The salt-pan photo on slide 82 is titled Salineras de Maras, based on its GPS metadata and pictured scene.

The show contains 258 selected photos: 214 appear alone, and 44 appear in 22 two-photo sequences. Four portrait pairs share the screen at nearly full height. The other pairs use a quick diagonal move within their shared frame over 5.5 seconds. Former filmstrip runs now play as individual full-size slides with a short dissolve between frames. The outgoing frame remains fully visible until the incoming image covers it, preventing a black gap. Every photo gets an unobstructed hold with all source edges visible. The camera AVIFs and remaining HEICs retain their original versions where supported; the iPhone photos now use Lightroom-exported JPEG originals.

Original HEIC, AVIF, and Lightroom-exported iPhone JPEG photos are kept in `assets/originals/`. The player offers these to capable browsers and uses SDR JPEGs in `assets/photos/` as fallbacks. In particular, the original 10-bit BT.2020/PQ AVIFs remain available for HDR displays. H.264 video versions in `assets/video/` keep playback reliable across browsers; the source clips remain untouched in `../Images/`. Each photo and video has a `rating` in the manifest; both photos in a paired frame have individual ratings in `items`. Media without an embedded star rating receives 3. The AVIF source `DSCF0717` is absent from `Images/`, so its existing `assets/originals/` copy stays in use with a default rating of 3.

The slideshow data lives in `manifest.json` and `manifest.js`. After editing `tools/build_manifest.py` or `tools/motion_plan.json`, rerun the builder from the parent directory to regenerate both manifests. `playback_plan.js` derives the Full and Quick frame lists, chapter positions, and music cues. Run `node Web/tools/test_playback_plan.js` after changing that logic. The motion plan gives people photos a gentle move toward detected faces, pans wide images by less than a percent, and holds scenic horizontal landscapes still. Other stills receive a 2.5% zoom in or out. These moves stay within the full fitted image, so no source edge is cropped. Motion pauses with the slideshow and is disabled when the browser requests reduced motion. `tools/analyze_photos.m` and `tools/create_motion_plan.py` document how the per-photo plan was generated locally. The complete site is static and can be committed to a GitHub Pages repository later without a build step.

## Music

The chosen recordings are bundled locally for offline playback. Each track has a cue anchored to an original frame in the manifest. Quick Show switches at that frame if it survives, or the next surviving frame. Seeking selects the track for the destination frame. For every music change, the outgoing track begins fading across the two preceding frames. The incoming track starts three seconds before its cue and fades up while the outgoing track is still audible; the handoff completes at the cue frame. Two audio elements allow the recordings to overlap. The final Andean recording is about 27 seconds shorter than the remaining Full Show; it fades out and begins a quiet reprise rather than jumping back to the first song.

The HAUSER handoff completes on Full frame 106, one frame before the Machu Picchu title at frame 107. The Andean Spirit handoff completes on Full frame 185, on the same Lake Titicaca image as before. In Quick Show those handoffs move to the corresponding retained frames.
